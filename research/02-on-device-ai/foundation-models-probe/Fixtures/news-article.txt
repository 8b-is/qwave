Local council votes on river restoration plan

By A. Reporter · 14 August 2026

The city council approved a three-year restoration plan for the lower river corridor on Tuesday evening, clearing the way for bank reinforcement work to begin this autumn.

The plan earmarks 4.2 million units for floodplain regrading, native planting along two kilometres of bank, and a new pedestrian bridge connecting the north and south trails. Council members voted 9-2 after a two-hour debate that featured fourteen public speakers.

"This is the first time the corridor has a single coordinated plan," said the planning lead, who called the vote a milestone. "The flood studies alone took eighteen months."

Opponents questioned the bridge's placement, arguing the crossing would disturb a heron rookery documented in a 2024 survey. The two dissenting votes both cited the survey.

The restored corridor is expected to host an additional forty thousand visitors per year, according to the city's modelling.

Construction is scheduled to start in October, weather permitting. A community open house is planned for September.
